The Art of Prophylaxis: Thinking Ahead Like Anatoly Karpov

By GM Alexander Ivanov
• October 28, 2026 • 8 Min Read

The 12th World Champion Anatoly Karpov was once asked what his core strategic principle was when sitting across the board from aggressive grandmasters like Garry Kasparov or Viktor Korchnoi. His answer was deceptively simple: "I look at my opponent’s position and ask myself: what does he want to do? Then I stop him from doing it."

1. Understanding the Difference Between Passive Defense and Prophylaxis

Amateur chess players frequently confuse defensive play with passivity. Passive play yields territory and allows the opponent to place their pieces on ideal attacking outposts without consequence. Prophylaxis, by contrast, is an active preventive strike. It denies the opponent their natural freeing moves (such as ...c5 in the French Defense or ...d5 in the Caro-Kann) before they can execute them.

2. The Three-Question Calculation Filter

Before touching any piece during your tournament rounds, train your subconscious to execute this three-step checklist:

Step 1: What is my opponent's primary threat on the next move?

Is there a piece hanging, a fork threatened, or a file about to be infiltrated?

Step 2: What is their intended strategic plan over the next 3 moves?

Are they preparing to reroute their knight to an outpost square or launch a kingside pawn storm?

Step 3: Can I combine prophylaxis with my own positional advance?

The hallmark of a master move is a move that stops enemy counterplay while simultaneously setting up your own offensive breakout.

3. Conclusion: Cultivating Patience on the 64 Squares

Rushing forward prematurely with attacks before securing your own king's sanctuary is the leading cause of sudden blunders in club chess. Integrate prophylactic habits into your weekly puzzle training and watch your opponent's attacking options dissolve into frustration.
